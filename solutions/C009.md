## Chapter 009

### EX-C009-01 — Count device pairs

Return 0 when the count is smaller than two. Widen to
64 bits and compute `n(n−1)/2`. The products represent
ordered pairs and every unordered pair has a unique pair
of orders. Inputs 2 and 6 yield 1 and 15. With the
largest 32-bit input, the product is less than
`(2^32)^2`, hence it fits in 64 bits and the answer
is 9,223,372,030,412,324,865. A calculation in 32 bits
can overflow before division.

### EX-C009-02 — Count passing samples

Let `g` be the count of good devices and `f` the
faulty count. Widen the counts before adding. Return
`(choose(g, 2), choose(g+f, 2))` when at least two
devices exist. The stated cases return `(6,15)`,
`(0,1)`, and absence. With replacement one simple
uniform space is ordered draws, giving `g²` passing
draws out of `(g+f)²`. Mixing an ordered numerator with
an unordered denominator leads to the wrong probability.

### EX-C009-03 — Advance a circular index

Reject invalid capacity or index first. Reduce the
advance modulo capacity. Compute the distance
`capacity − index`. If the step is smaller, add
it to the index. Otherwise subtract the distance from
the step. Both paths return the unique canonical
remainder by the proof in the chapter. Capacity one
forces both valid index and result to zero. For index 6
with capacity 8, advance 2 gives zero. At capacity
`u64::MAX`, index `u64::MAX−1` and advance 2
give 1, without overflowing the type. Award credit for
the exact-boundary comparison, including equality.

### EX-C009-04 — Compute a whole-number GCD

While `b > 0`, compute `r = a mod b` and replace
`(a,b)` with `(b,r)`. Return `a`. The four
example results are 6, 6, 17, and 1. For two zeros
return zero by definition. A reference for inputs
other than `(0,0)` can search divisors 1 through
`max(a,b)` and remember the largest for which both
remainders are zero. This choice also handles a zero
argument. The test oracle is slower and shares no
remainder-reduction recurrence with the implementation.
The algorithm's decreasing argument establishes termination.

### EX-C009-05 — Derive a three-digit code space

There are nine choices for the first digit, nine remaining
choices for the second, and eight for the third. The product
is 648. Enumerate first digits 1 through 9 in ascending
order, then second digits 0 through 9 skipping the first,
then third digits 0 through 9 skipping both earlier
digits. Emit `100a + 10b + c` for digits `a, b, c`.
Digit uniqueness makes each generated code distinct and
the nested ascending order gives numeric order. The triple
`(1,0,2)` is accepted and `(1,0,1)` fails
the final constraint. To sample uniformly, each stage can
choose uniformly from exactly its permitted set. A
generator that weights particular first digits differently
can produce 648 possible codes and still be biased.

### Interview answers for Chapter 009

**IQ-C009-01.** Write the failure count as two zero-or-one
indicators and distribute the finite weighted sum. This
requires the marginal probabilities. Independence would let
you multiply unconditional probabilities for a joint event,
which is a different computation.

**IQ-C009-02.** The invariant establishes the meaning of any
final pair. The algorithm still needs a decreasing measure
to reach a final pair, and the final condition `b = 0`
explains why `a` is the greatest divisor of both. A loop
that preserves its original state could preserve the common
divisors forever.
