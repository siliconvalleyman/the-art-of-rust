## Integrated practice

Use this assessment after Chapters 6–10. Write complete
arguments and justify the domain of each formula. The
five problems are worth four points each. Attempt them
before consulting the answer key.

1. **Safety and completeness, intermediate.** In a batch
   of at least one nonnegative record length, an implementation
   accepts on the first length that fits a byte. The desired
   rule accepts exactly when every length fits. Specify the
   incorrect and desired predicates, produce a counterexample,
   and fix the rule. State the behavior on an empty batch
   when the specification is extended.
2. **Composition and witnesses, intermediate.** Vertices
   A–D have directed edges A→B, A→C, B→D, and C→D.
   Give a two-step decision and both witnesses from A to D.
   Change exactly one edge so only one witness remains.
   Give a reason why a walk of length one is insufficient
   evidence for the two-step rule.
3. **Induction and bounded integers, challenge.** Show by
   induction that the sum of the first `n` positive odd
   integers is `n²`, taking the empty sum as zero.
   For an unsigned 32-bit input, state the smaller of
   unsigned 32-bit or unsigned 64-bit storage that can
   return every sum in the domain.
4. **Counting and expectation, intermediate.** There are
   three bad devices and five good devices. Two different
   devices are chosen uniformly without replacement. Find
   the probability of at least one bad device and the expected
   number of bad devices. Specify the sample space and
   justify both calculations.
5. **Amortized work, challenge.** Suppose an array starts
   with zero capacity, grows to one on the first insertion,
   then triples when full. Copying or writing an item costs
   one unit. Find the exact copy count for ten insertions,
   give an upper bound proportional to the number of
   insertions, and identify which insertion costs most
   among the first ten.
