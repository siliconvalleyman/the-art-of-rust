### EX-C013-01 — Widen before taking an average

**Beginner.** Given two unsigned bytes, return the floor
of their ordinary average as another byte. Test `(0,0)`,
`(3,4)`, `(250,20)`, and `(255,255)`. Justify the
range of the widened sum and of the result.

### EX-C013-02 — Specify a wrapped sequence number

**Beginner.** Advance a byte sequence number by a byte
increment. Its required result is the low byte of the
ordinary sum. Test 254 plus 1, 254 plus 2, and 255 plus
255. Explain why conversion of a sufficiently widened
sum to a byte can implement the same policy.

### EX-C013-03 — Reject a truncating length conversion

**Intermediate.** A protocol length is read as `u16`
and stored in a byte field only when it fits. Implement
the conversion with a result that distinguishes failure.
Test 0, 255, and 256. Give a reason why matching the low
byte would accept the wrong payload length at the boundary.

### EX-C013-04 — Require an exact byte-valued measurement

**Intermediate.** Accept a floating-point input as a byte
only if the input is finite, integral, and within 0
through 255. Regard negative zero as numerically equal to
zero. Test `-0.0`, `12.5`, `255.0`, `255.5`,
`-1.0`, infinity, and NaN. Explain why checking the cast
result alone cannot establish the input's validity.

### EX-C013-05 — Find a lost identifier

**Challenge.** Write a test that exposes two consecutive
distinct `u64` identifiers becoming equal as `f64`.
Use the first integer above `2^53` and its predecessor.
Compare both the original integers and the converted
values, then state a representation choice that meets
an exact-identifier requirement.

- **IQ-C013-02, intermediate.** Why can checking whether
